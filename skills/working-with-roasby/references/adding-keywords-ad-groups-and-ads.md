# Adding keywords, ad groups and ads

## Keywords

Call `preview_add_keywords` with the `ad_group_id` (an ad group of a Google Ads or Microsoft Ads search campaign; ChatGPT Ads has no keywords) and `keywords`, each with `text` (any language, without match type symbols), `match_type` (`exact`, `phrase` or `broad`), and optionally `cpc_bid_micros` (only when the campaign uses manual bids, within the account's max bid when one is set; without it the ad group's bid applies) and an https `final_url`. Roasby checks each keyword on its own and refuses only the ones that fail, with their reason: too long (80 characters and 10 words on Google Ads, 100 characters on Microsoft Ads), characters Google refuses, the same keyword twice, a keyword the ad group already has (enabled or paused; a removed one can be added again), a negative keyword on the ad group or its campaign that blocks it (a broad negative blocks keywords with all its words, a phrase negative those with its words in order, an exact one the same words; the `detail` names the negative: the user reverts the Roasby change that added it or takes it away in the ad platform, or leaves the keyword out), text written with `[brackets]` or in `"quotes"` (`keyword_match_type_symbols`: the match type goes in `match_type`), the max bid once one is set (`above_max_bid`), automated bidding. Show the user the keywords that will be added and every refused one with its reason, then call `add_keywords` with the same arguments and the token. One add runs at a time per ad group. Every keyword is created paused, even in an enabled ad group: tell the user, and once the batch is applied offer to enable exactly those keywords (their `entity_id`s from `list_ad_changes` with the `batch_id`) through `preview_enable_entities` and one more confirmation. Reverting the batch pauses the keywords it added (refused as `keywords_still_paused` while none was enabled); there is no removing them.

## Ad groups

Call `preview_add_ad_groups` with the `campaign_id` (a Google Ads or Microsoft Ads search campaign; ChatGPT Ads is refused) and `ad_groups` (up to 100 in one call), each with a `name` (any language, unique in the campaign: at most 254 characters on Google Ads, 255 on Microsoft Ads), a `cpc_bid_micros` default bid (required when the campaign uses manual bids, within the account's max bid when one is set; refused under automated bidding), and optionally `keywords` (shaped like `add_keywords`, up to 1,000), `negative_keywords` (shaped like `add_negative_keywords`, up to 100) and up to 3 `responsive_search_ads` (each shaped like every new ad, below, with an optional `final_url_suffix`). It is the same ad group shape as a new campaign's `ad_groups`. Without an ad, the ad group shows no ads: ask the user for one. Roasby checks each ad group with all its parts and refuses only the ones that fail, with their reason and the part that breaks it (`detail`, such as `keyword: shoes! (Broad match)`): the name (too long, `duplicate_ad_group_name` in the campaign, `duplicate_ad_group_in_request`), `too_many_ad_groups` (20,000 per campaign), the bid (`ad_group_bid_required`, `above_max_bid`, `bids_automated`), a keyword that `add_keywords` would refuse, the same keyword twice in it (`duplicate_keyword_in_ad_group`), a keyword with the same text as its own negative (`negative_blocks_keyword`) or a campaign negative, a broad match negative on Microsoft Ads. Every ad group, keyword, negative keyword and ad counts toward the items of one batch (`batch_items`). Show the user the ad groups that will be added (with their counts of keywords, negatives and ads) and every refused one with its reason, then call `add_ad_groups` with the same arguments and the token. One add runs at a time per campaign. Every ad group is created paused, even in an enabled campaign, with its keywords and ads enabled under it: tell the user, and once the batch is applied offer to enable exactly those ad groups (their `entity_id`s from `list_ad_changes` with the `batch_id`) through `preview_enable_entities`; enabling an ad group starts it only while its campaign runs. On Microsoft Ads an ad group is made step by step: a failed later step leaves it paused and partly made (the item fails with the reason), and Roasby won't enable it (`ad_group_incomplete`) until a sync shows an ad in it: the user finishes it in Microsoft Ads. Ad groups have no start or end dates on Google Ads (only campaigns do), so Roasby sets none. Reverting the batch pauses the ad groups it added (refused as `ad_groups_still_paused` while none was enabled); there is no removing them.

## Responsive search ad rules

Every new responsive search ad (a new campaign's, a new ad group's, or one added to an ad group) has one shape and one set of rules: `final_url` (https), 3 to 15 `headlines` of up to 30 characters and 2 to 4 `descriptions` of up to 90, each an object `{"text": "...", "pinned_position": 1}` (`pinned_position` optional: headlines 1 to 3, descriptions 1 to 2), optional `path1` and `path2` (up to 15, no spaces or slashes, `path2` only with `path1`). Characters of Chinese, Japanese and Korean, full-width characters and emoji count as two, so such a headline holds 15 of them. Headlines must differ from each other (letter case and spacing don't count), and so must descriptions. Pin only text that must show: pinning lowers ad strength, and pinned ones show only in their position, so keep at least as many unpinned headlines as headline positions (1 to 3) nothing is pinned to, and the same for descriptions (1 to 2); otherwise the ad is refused as `pinning_leaves_position_empty` (two headlines pinned to 1 and one unpinned leaves positions 2 and 3 one headline for two places). A refused ad names the part (`detail`, such as `headline 4: ...`); the codes are `headline_count`, `description_count`, `headline_too_long`, `description_too_long`, `duplicate_headline`, `duplicate_description`, `pinning_leaves_position_empty`, `display_path_too_long`, `invalid_display_path`, `invalid_final_url`, `invalid_final_url_suffix`.

## Ads in existing ad groups

Call `preview_add_responsive_search_ads` with `ads` (up to 100 in one call), each with its `ad_group_id` (an ad group of a Google Ads or Microsoft Ads search campaign; all of one call in one ad account, else `mixed_ad_accounts`; ChatGPT Ads is refused as `ads_not_supported`), the ad, and optionally a `final_url_suffix` (tracking parameters such as `utm_source=google`, no leading `?`). Ask the user for the headlines, descriptions and landing page, and whether anything must be pinned. Roasby refuses only the ads that fail, with their reason and part, plus an ad group of a removed or non-search campaign and a Microsoft Ads ad group that holds 100 ads (`too_many_ads_in_ad_group`). Show the user the ads (first headline, counts, pins) and the refused ones, then call `add_responsive_search_ads` with the same arguments and the token. One add runs at a time per ad group. Every ad is created paused, even in an enabled ad group: tell the user, and once the batch is applied offer to enable exactly those ads (their `entity_id`s from `list_ad_changes` with the `batch_id`) through `preview_enable_entities`. An ad group runs at most 3 enabled responsive search ads, so enabling a fourth is refused (`too_many_enabled_ads`): offer to pause one of the others first. The ad platform still reviews each ad: one it refuses under its policies fails with the reason in plain words; Roasby never asks for a policy exception, so tell the user to change the wording, or to ask the ad platform for a review on its own website. Reverting the batch pauses the ads it added (refused as `ads_still_paused` while none was enabled); there is no removing them.
