# Campaign settings

To change an existing Google Ads or Microsoft Ads search campaign's settings, read its current `settings` in `list_ad_entities` (entity_type campaign), then call `preview_update_campaign_settings` with its `campaign_id` and only the sections to change: `countries` (the whole set of ISO codes; excluded places stay), `places` (the whole set of regions, cities, metro areas and counties by `location_id` from `search_locations`, and radiuses as `latitude`, `longitude` and `radius_km`; read the current ones in `settings.places`; only what differs is taken away or added; the preview names each new place by the ad platform's own name, refused with place_not_available for an ID the platform does not target and with locations_required when neither countries nor places are left, and with `locations_not_only_countries` when the campaign also targets a location group or an address), `location_option`, search_partners (true or false; Microsoft Ads sets it on every ad group), `final_url_suffix` and `tracking_url_template` (null clears one), `ad_schedule` (the whole schedule of `{day, start, end, bid_adjustment_percent}` in quarter hours, in `settings.time_zone`; an empty list runs all day; at most 6 slots a day on Google Ads, 7 a day and 49 in all on Microsoft Ads), `device_bid_modifiers` (percent per device; -100 for no ads on it), `ad_rotation` (`optimize` or `rotate_indefinitely`, set on every ad group) and, on Microsoft Ads only, `languages`. Google Ads search campaigns have no language targeting: languages are refused there (`no_language_targeting`), so explain that Google matches languages itself. Show the user every line of `details` (each section before and after); all sections are one change. Bid adjustments count like bids, per device and hour: for every device and quarter hour that shows ads after the change, the device multiplier times the hour's multiplier (+20% is 1.2) may rise at most the account's max bid change in one step from what that pair had before. A device or hour that showed no ads before (a device at -100%, or hours a schedule left out) counts as 0% and its pairs may not rise above that in the same step; no schedule means every hour at 0%. Otherwise it is refused (`bid_change_too_large`, naming the device and hours in `details`), unless you pass `limit_to_guardrails: true`: then tell the user what was limited (taking a schedule away may wait for a later step), and once the change is applied preview exactly the `next_step` it gives. Lowering and turning a device off are always allowed. `no_ad_groups_to_set` means the ad rotation (or, on Microsoft Ads, search partners) was asked for a campaign with no synced ad groups: sync first. A section someone changed in the ad platform since Roasby's last sync is not sent (the change log names it and Roasby syncs again): preview again from the new values. When new countries include EU countries on Google Ads and the campaign has no EU political ads declaration, `user_actions` asks the user to declare it in Google Ads. `settings_not_synced` means run `run_ad_account_sync` first. Reverting the change restores every section it changed (`changed_since` when one changed again); your revert of a lowered adjustment must also fit the max bid change step. A Performance Max campaign (Google Ads or Microsoft Ads) takes only `countries`, `location_option`, `ad_schedule` (every `bid_adjustment_percent` 0), `final_url_suffix` and `tracking_url_template`: anything else is refused with `setting_not_on_campaign_type`, so leave it out. A Google Ads display or shopping campaign takes the same sections plus `device_bid_modifiers` and schedule bid adjustments. A Microsoft Ads shopping campaign takes those too, plus `languages`. A Microsoft Ads audience campaign (listed as display) takes only `final_url_suffix` and `tracking_url_template`: its targeting is in its ad groups. Video, app and Microsoft Ads hotel campaigns are refused (`settings_not_on_campaign_type`): the user changes them in the ad platform.

## Meta ad set targeting

A Meta Ads ad set's targeting changes with `preview_update_meta_targeting` and then `update_meta_targeting` with the same arguments and the token: its `ad_group_id` and only the parts that change, each replaced as a whole. Read its current targeting first in `list_ad_entities` (`meta_targeting`, with the keys and IDs of what it targets), so a part keeps what the user wants to keep:

- `locations`: `countries` (two-letter codes), `regions` and `cities` by key, a city with an optional `radius_km` (17 to 80). Zips, places and excluded areas stay as they are.
- `languages` by key, `gender` (`all`, `male`, `female`), `age_min` with `age_max`, `interests` by ID.
- `audiences`: the `included` and `excluded` custom and lookalike audience IDs.
- `placements`: `automatic` true, or `platforms` (facebook, instagram, audience_network, messenger, threads) and their positions.

Find keys and IDs with `search_meta_targeting`: `type` `location`, `interest` or `language` with a `query` (read from Meta live), or `audience` (the account's audiences as Roasby read them). Tell the user that Meta may restart the ad set's learning, and, when the ad set uses Advantage+ audience, that ages, gender and interests are only suggestions there. Refusals: targeting_not_synced (sync the account first), interests_not_editable (its detailed targeting has more than one group of interests: change it in Meta Ads Manager), audience_not_available, special_category_targeting (a housing, employment or financial products campaign takes all ages from 18, all genders and a city radius of at least 25 km), eu_payer_required (give `dsa_payor` and `dsa_beneficiary`). A targeting changed in Meta since the last sync is not sent. A revert puts back only the parts the change changed.
