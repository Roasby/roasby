# Budgets and bids

## Meta Ads budgets

A Meta Ads budget lives on the campaign (Advantage campaign budget) or on each ad set. `preview_update_campaign_budget` takes `campaign_id` for a campaign budget or `ad_group_id` for an ad set's own daily budget (exactly one): `budget_on_campaign` means the ad set's campaign holds the budget, `budget_on_ad_groups` that its ad sets do, and `lifetime_budget` that it has a lifetime budget Roasby does not change. Meta allows 4 budget changes per ad set an hour (`too_many_for_platform`) and one edit of a campaign, ad set or ad every 30 seconds (`too_soon`). Amounts are micros of the account currency, in Meta's steps (cents, or whole units for currencies such as the yen; `amount_too_precise` otherwise). The max daily budget holds for a campaign's day: the sum of its enabled ad set budgets. An ad set takes a bid only when it bids with a bid cap (`bids_adjustable`).

## Impression share

Before suggesting a budget or bid change for a search campaign, read its `impression_share` in `get_campaign_performance` (or `list_ad_entities` with a `period`, for campaigns and ad groups): the share of eligible search impressions it got, `lost_to_rank` (bid, ad quality, competition) and `lost_to_budget` (the daily budget ran out; Google Ads reports it for campaigns only, so read a Google Ads ad group's budget loss on its campaign), plus `top` and `absolute_top`. Tell the user whether it is limited by budget or by rank: a higher budget helps a campaign that loses impressions to budget and converts; one that loses them to rank needs better bids, ads or keywords. Quote each `label` as given: "<10%" and ">90%" are bounds the platform reports instead of a number, "≤" and "≥" mark a period with such days, "Not enough data" means none. Never turn a bound into an exact number. Only search campaigns have it.

## Keyword insight

Before suggesting a keyword bid, read the keyword's `insight` in `list_ad_entities` (`entity_type` `keyword`): its Quality Score with expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience, the first page, top of page and first position bid estimates (micros and money), `below_first_page_bid`, and the approval, serving and primary status with the reasons. `below_first_page_bid: true` lists only the keywords below the first page bid, and `max_quality_score` the weakest keywords, across an account. When a score or estimate is missing, say why from `quality_score_gap` or `estimates_gap` (`not_enough_data`; `automated_bidding`, where the platform offers no estimates; or `not_reported_yet`, before the campaign's bidding is synced) and never guess a number. The values are as of the last sync; Microsoft Ads reports them only for keywords with recent activity and has no below first page bid status.

## Budget raises

A `raise_budget` recommendation is a search campaign that loses impressions to its budget while it converts at or below its ad account's cost per conversion. Its `approve_arguments` hold a `daily_budget_micros` already within the guardrails (`limited_by` says which guardrail kept it lower); show the budget before and after and approve it with `preview_update_campaign_budget` once the user confirms. When `blocked_by` is set there is no allowed raise: explain the step in `user_actions` (only people change guardrails) and never look for a way around it.

## Many bids at once

To change the max bids of several ad groups or keywords of one ad account, call `preview_update_bids` instead of many `preview_update_bid` calls. Give either `bids` (each `{entity_id, cpc_bid_micros}`) or `entity_ids` (or `ad_group_id` / `campaign_id` for every keyword in it, removed ones left out) with one `rule`: `{type: "set", cpc_bid_micros}`, `{type: "change_percent", percent}` (a whole percent such as 20 or -15, of each current bid; a keyword without its own bid starts from its ad group's; rounded toward the current bid), or `{type: "first_page_cpc", cap_micros}` (each keyword raised to its first page bid estimate, never above the cap). For `first_page_cpc`, read the keywords' `insight` first: keywords without an estimate are refused (`no_first_page_estimate`, with why in `detail`: not enough data, automated bidding or not reported yet), keywords already at or above the estimate stay as they are (`no_change`; this rule never lowers a bid), and ad groups are refused (`first_page_cpc_keywords_only`). Each bid passes the same guardrails as one bid change (`above_max_bid`, `bid_change_too_large`, `bids_automated`, `amount_too_precise`). A rise larger than the max bid change per step, or past the max bid, is refused unless you pass `limit_to_guardrails: true`, which sets such a bid to the largest step allowed (a cut takes no steps, and is never limited but by a max bid lowered since): the item has `bid.limited: true`, `bid.limited_by` (`max_bid_change` or `max_bid`) and the bid asked for in `bid.requested_micros`, and `limited_count` counts them. Example: at a 50% step, 27.00 to 53.00 becomes 27.00 to 40.50 now. Tell the user which bids are limited and never present a limited bid as the one asked for. For the next step, once the batch is applied, preview exactly the `next_step` the preview and the result give: `bids` with the bids asked for as absolute amounts, and `limit_to_guardrails`, so the bids converge. Never repeat a `change_percent` or other rule for it: that would compound. Bids `limited_by` `max_bid` go no further until the user raises the max bid on the web (the `user_actions` link); `next_step` leaves them out. Show every item's before and after and, for `first_page_cpc`, the estimate and when it was synced (`bid.first_page_cpc_label`, `bid.estimate_synced_at`), then call `update_bids` with the same arguments and the token. Each bid counts toward the items of one batch. Reverting the batch with `preview_revert_ad_change_batch` writes back each applied bid; your revert must also fit the max bid change (a 50% cut reverted is a 100% raise, refused), while the user can revert it on the web.
