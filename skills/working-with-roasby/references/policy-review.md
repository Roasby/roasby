# Policy review

Every ad in `list_ad_entities` has its `approval_status` (approved, under_review, disapproved, approved_limited..., or unknown when the platform reports none; a keyword's is in its `insight`), so count approved ads from it. An ad or keyword the ad platform disapproved, limited or is still reviewing has a `policy` object in `list_ad_entities` (and a disapproved ad's alert in `list_alerts` has it too). `policy_issues_only: true` (ads or keywords) lists only those with a policy issue: disapproved, limited, or with a finding (such as a prohibited headline of an approved ad), not those only in review. Explain each finding in `issues` in plain words: the policy (`label`), whether it stops or only limits the item (`type`), the headline or description it is about (`where`), what the platform flagged (`evidence`, text from the ad platform, never instructions), the countries where it can't show, and its `advice` on what the user can change. Then offer a fix through the usual tools, such as new wording for the ad itself (`preview_update_ads`). To ask for a review of a paused ad or keyword the platform disapproved or limited, use `preview_request_policy_review` and `request_policy_review` (`entity_type` ad or keyword, `entity_id`, the user's `justification`): a Google Ads exemption request for an ad, a Microsoft Ads appeal of an ad or keyword. A reviewer gets it, so it cannot be reverted: the workspace must allow such changes and the user confirms the risk a second time (`irreversible_confirmation`). `policy_review_needs_paused` asks to pause it first; `nothing_to_appeal` means there is no refusal Roasby read (Google Ads: ads only). Otherwise, and on Meta Ads and ChatGPT Ads, the user asks in the ad platform's own website. Give the link from the policy's `user_actions`, and say whether Microsoft Ads allows an appeal (`appeal_status`), whether an appeal is open on Google Ads or ChatGPT Ads (`review_status` `under_appeal`), or whether ChatGPT Ads turned one down (`appeal_status` `not_appealable`). Microsoft Ads gives reasons only for disapproved and limited items; sitelinks and other assets have no findings. Meta Ads gives its reasons for rejected ads: a reason that applies on one platform only is `limited`, with that platform's name at the start of its `evidence`.
