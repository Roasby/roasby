# Bidding strategy

To change how a Google Ads or Microsoft Ads search, Performance Max or shopping campaign, or a Google Ads display campaign, bids, read its `bidding` in `list_ad_entities` (entity_type campaign: `strategy`, targets, `changeable`: false for Google Ads enhanced cost per click or another strategy Roasby could not set back, so a change of it cannot be reverted, needs the workspace setting and a second confirmation as `irreversible_confirmation`, and the user should know it stays), then call `preview_update_bidding` with its `campaign_id` and a `strategy`: `manual_cpc` (Microsoft Ads: enhanced cost per click), `maximize_clicks`, `maximize_conversions` (optional `target_cpa_micros`), `maximize_conversion_value` (optional `target_roas_percent`, 350 for 350%) or `target_impression_share` (`impression_share_location`, `impression_share_percent`, `max_cpc_micros`). A Google Ads ad group under maximize conversions with a campaign target CPA (and the campaign's own strategy, not a portfolio) takes its own target CPA: `ad_group_id` with `target_cpa_micros` (null takes it away, always allowed). A pending ad group target CPA shows as the ad group's `has_pending_bid_change`. Maximize clicks takes no ceiling: one the campaign has is kept while the strategy stays (also under maximize conversions or conversion value). Target impression share needs all three of its inputs (`impression_share_incomplete`). Before suggesting maximize conversions or conversion value, check the conversion tracking (`list_conversion_actions`): when the campaign's goals record nothing, or for value Roasby has seen no conversion value, the preview is still allowed with a warning line; tell the user the bidding has nothing to aim for until the tracking is fixed in the ad platform. Show the user every `details` line: automated bidding sets the bids itself (bid changes are refused while it runs: `bids_automated`), the learning period of one to two weeks, a strategy without a target spends up to the daily budget, and the guardrails. Each guardrail holds only once a person set it: a max cost per click stays within the account's max bid (`target_above_max_bid`); a target CPA within the account's max target CPA (`target_above_max_target_cpa`), never within the max bid; a target ROAS at or above the account's min target ROAS (`target_below_min_target_roas`); `list_ad_accounts` shows both; in one step a target CPA or max cost per click rises, and a target ROAS falls, at most the max bid change (`target_change_too_large`), unless you pass `limit_to_guardrails: true`: then tell the user what was limited and, once applied, preview the `bidding_next_step` it gives. A target the campaign has none of now is measured from the most recent one Roasby set or took away, so taking a target away (here or in the ad platform, or by switching strategy) and setting it again keeps the step; only a target with no such history is a first target (no step; a first target ROAS gets a plain warning). On Microsoft Ads, target impression share is not changed yet (`impression_share_not_supported`): the user does that in Microsoft Ads. `bidding_not_revertible` means Roasby could not set the bidding back (a portfolio strategy, Google enhanced CPC, another strategy): the user changes it in the ad platform. `bidding_not_synced` means run `run_ad_account_sync` first. A Performance Max campaign takes only `maximize_conversions` or `maximize_conversion_value` (each with its optional target), a shopping campaign `maximize_clicks` or `maximize_conversion_value`, and a Google Ads display campaign `maximize_clicks`, `maximize_conversions` or `maximize_conversion_value` (`strategy_not_on_campaign_type` otherwise). A Microsoft Ads audience campaign (listed as display) takes no bidding change (`strategy_not_on_campaign_type`): the user changes it in Microsoft Ads. A revert restores the exact strategy and targets (except the Microsoft Ads max cost per click above). Your revert stays within the guardrails and the max bid change per step (refused past them as revert past guardrails); tell the user they can revert it on the Change log page on the web, which restores the exact earlier target. An account not read yet (`synced_at` null) or one Roasby can't read (`readable` false, ChatGPT Ads) has nothing to judge, and one not read recently (`stale` true) may be out of date: say so instead of guessing. A goal Roasby can't read (`unknown_action_selected`) is not a removed one: ask the user to check it in the ad platform.

For a short event, `preview_add_bid_adjustment` and `apply_change` create a seasonality adjustment (`type` seasonality, `conversion_rate_change_percent`) or a data exclusion (`type` data_exclusion, for a tracking outage) for some campaigns, at most 14 days, in the account's time zone. `preview_add_campaign_label` and `apply_change` put a new label on a campaign. A revert deletes the label, seasonality adjustment or data exclusion that change created (only what Roasby made).

Portfolio bid strategies: `preview_create_portfolio_strategy` and `apply_change` create one, unused until a campaign joins; deleting one is permanent, so it cannot be reverted and needs the workspace setting and `irreversible_confirmation`. A search campaign joins one Roasby created in that ad account with `preview_update_bidding` and `portfolio_id` (the ID in the change log of its creation; `portfolio_not_roasbys` for any other) instead of `strategy`; joining and leaving are reverted exactly.
